# General W3C Open Source Program Policies

**Status**: Draft.

This page documents information intended to be generally applicable across Projects in the W3C Open Source Program.

References to “W3C” as a decision making entity mean “W3C Staff”. This document does not cover details of how the W3C Management allocates decision-making within the Staff.

## Participation

Anyone involved in a Project in any capacity (including Contributors) must adhere to the [W3C Code of Conduct](https://www.w3.org/policies/code-of-conduct/) and the [W3C Antitrust and Competition Policy](https://www.w3.org/policies/antitrust-2024/).

W3C expects Projects to operate in public.

## Lifecycle of a Project

**Note**: Work is ongoing for a more complete definition of the lifecycle of Projects in this Program.

### Ongoing monitoring of Project health

W3C staff will monitor the health of the Project and if activity levels drop below “healthy” work with the Project to identify a path to renewed energy.

### Continuing / Discontinuing a Project

W3C Staff expects to continue the project as long as the minimum resource requirements are available.

W3C Staff may make certain commitments contingent upon a minimum level of resource commitment from a clearly identified set of Supporters.

W3C Staff will make any decisions to discontinue hosting the Project (and winding it down or exporting it in a responsible way). 

## Legal

W3C will be responsible for decisions and policies related to Project intellectual property, including logos, trademarks, and domains. 

W3C will provide means for public input both about the open W3C Open Source Program and to specific Projects.

## Staff resource allocation

W3C will be responsible for any decisions related to the allocation of staff resources or W3C infrastructure.

## Brand and communications

W3C will make decisions and establish all policies related to the use of the W3C brand and logo in conjunction with the Project.

## Financial management

W3C will make decisions and establish all policies for accepting cash and/or in-kind services for Projects, ensuring in particular that all contributions are handled in a manner consistent with W3C’s 501(c)(3) status, and in alignment with W3C policies and procedures. Furthermore:

* Before approaching prospective Supporters (philanthropic organizations or individuals, sponsors, etc), W3C should be notified, so that due diligence can be completed.  Staff should be part of the conversations with prospective Supporters very early in those discussions. Contributions will be accepted in accordance with W3C fundraising policy and managed with established processes and practices.

* W3C will establish clear processes and requirements for decisions to allocate funding to individuals.

W3C has a long history of decoupling Membership dues from direct control over strategic and technical decisions. In that spirit, W3C also seeks to decouple contributions from direct control over strategic and technical decisions of Open Source Projects. 

* W3C is authorized to reserve 20% of any contributions received to cover expenses (e.g., communications, legal, systems, billing, open source program office, etc.).

* In the interest of transparency, general reporting of how contributions are managed will be shared with the community regularly. Funds received will be audited annually as part of the W3C audit.
